**Max Zhou**

Co-Founder at MetaApp | LinkedIn PowerProfile 2018

![图片](https://mmbiz.qpic.cn/mmbiz_png/ERHx76pYjiau9gAb0iav362EeJpTa62TIBD88AHEnVoicS2lNcKSHvTFUEFNC20QP1n5XN9iauRrBVWJN6OaRvvfNw/640?wx_fmt=png)

Each year, graduating HBS students are asked to answer the question from the last lines of the poem “The Summer Day” by Pulitzer Prize-winning author, Mary Oliver, "What is it you plan to do with your one wild and precious life?" Ever since I came across this question, it never stopped lingering on my mind. After three years of constant struggle, here is my answer.

My life has been forever changed by Silicon Valley, a place where so much excitement around innovation, progress and wealth creation gets packed into.

After jumping on the journey of starting a startup myself, I would sometimes just go completely speechless. In those moments, what was I thinking? Later I realized: I was struck by the sheer force that resides in the palm of my hand.

To put it simply: I’ve discovered a superpower.

Steve Jobs describes it more eloquently: “Life can be so much broader, once you discover one simple fact, and that is that everything around you that you call 'life' was made up by people who were no smarter than you. And you can change it, you can influence it, you can build your own things that other people can use.”

I couldn’t agree more.

He discovered this fact four years after he first started Apple. When he visited a classroom of primary school kids playing with Apple II computers, all of them were so smooth and proficient. He realized that this little project he started a few years back will now change the way this group of children grows up, forever. This, in turn, means that millions of more children, and even generations of people, will be changed, and probably for the better. As a hustler selling blue boxes, Steve would never have imagined this much impact that a college dropout could make.

He had thought, the world just happens and “there were some pretty bright people running it." But here's the thing. "As you start to interact with some of these people, you find they're not a lot different than you."

Boom… Superpower!

With great power comes great responsibility. The people I look up to, the Peter Thiels, the Reid Hoffmans, and the Elon Musks, they’re not just profiteers of the internet evolution (like Mark Cuban and most Chinese internet millionaires). They are missionaries, not mercenaries. They build to last, not build to flip. They have strong convictions, and money is just a by-product of their success.

What was my North Star? I’ve written an article three years ago that my goal is to “build a startup company in China to better people's lives”. Today, I’ve done the first half of that sentence, building a startup company called MetaApp in China. We’ve closed the Series-B, and are now servicing an hour of content for millions of users every day. Can’t say I’ve mastered it, but there’s a sweet taste of the “Superpower” that Steve Jobs experienced back then.

What now?&nbsp;

Firstly, have more of it.

As a growth hacker, I’ve seen firsthand how amazing super-linear growth is, and how even the most experienced players can’t wrap their heads around the velocity. My goal is for MetaApp to grow to its fullest form and potentially redesign the eco-system of mobile internet. It excites as much as it frustrates from time to time. The hard thing about hard things is the emotional roller-coaster between epiphany and agony. Looking back, looking ahead, I would benefit immensely by having steady hands and strong nerves. No fear, no greed. Easier said than done.&nbsp;

Even if the company goes bust, I’ll still be destined on this path, knowing how special this Superpower is. The PayPal mafia is successful in starting subsequent businesses, according to Peter Thiel, because the PayPal journey is both grand enough and difficult enough, leaving the founding team with realistic high hopes (not cynical nor lunatic) long after they’ve left the company. Simply by knowing that it’s possible, and knowing that it’s incredibly difficult, will be sufficient to support me down this path.

Secondly, use it for good. Leverage it for the thing I’m most passionate about: the mission to influence and educate.

Between the co-founders, we’ve talked about how this little project can become our life’s work if it reaches over a certain scale. What is it do we wish MetaApp to become after it’s already sizeable? Garbage app for garbage men? No. Even when the whole world is dragging me down, I’ll still fight back, and use the power for good. It might be directly or indirectly, but I’ll garner the impact and release it towards a tiny objective. Let the unstoppable force break any immovable barrier!

As Sen, my co-founder, aptly asserts: the ultimate state in an individual's life is planting one's ideas in other people’s heads and putting their money in one's pocket in the process. Obviously a joke, but how awesome is that!

My goal after reaching the totem pole will be a mission to influence and educate, to bring more of my fellow citizens above the level of ignorance and live a transcendent life. That sounds a bit condescending, but let me break it down. For thousands of years, the majority of human society doesn’t get to have stories. Peasants are trapped on the farmland, workers are trapped in their “job loop”. The salvation would be for people to escape their daily routine, finally look up to the sky and think about meanings and purpose. Science and engineering help with the first half, while the Liberal Arts propel the second half. For each person to live an eventful and storied life, that’s my definition of progress for our civilization.

Lastly, leverage the Superpower to shape our society's superstructure.

One of the most important points in Sam Altman's recent article&nbsp;http://blog.samaltman.com/how-to-be-successful&nbsp;is "Be internally driven". To quote him, "most people are primarily externally driven; they do what they do because they want to impress other people. This is bad for many reasons". Every goal of mine as of late has two descriptions, an external one and an internal one. The first definition explains the goal to other folks, while the second definition supplies the "why".

I've always had this ambition to devote my every breath to a cause so grandiose that it's almost impossible to reach while so incredible if realized. My ultimate career goal using an external definition would be to become a vice-chairman of the Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference. The Chinese society is still rudimentary in its political discourse. I would like to pitch in my share of voice. Internally, this means creating an immense impact on the superstructure of the society and potentially reshape it; change the world as we know it for the better.

By my rough calculation, I'll spend my 20s and 30s in the first stage, my 40s and onward in the second and third stages. I'll end my article by the most powerful quote on this topic by Nikolai Ostrovsky:

*“Man's dearest possession is life. It is given to him but once, and he must live it so as to feel no torturing regrets for wasted years, never know the burning shame of a mean and petty past; so live that, dying, he might say: all my life, all my strength were given to the finest cause in all the world*──*the fight for the Liberation of Mankind”*
